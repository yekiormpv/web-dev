Why <label>?

The HTML <label> tag is used to provide a text explanation for form controls (like text boxes, checkboxes, or radio buttons) while drastically improving accessibility and usability.(cit. pēc W3Schools)

Google explained:

Why We Use It (The 3 Main Benefits)

  1. Larger Clickable Target (Usability): If you don't use a <label>, the user has to click precisely on the tiny checkbox square to toggle it. By wrapping or linking the text with a <label>, clicking anywhere on the words "I agree to the terms and conditions" will automatically check or uncheck the box. This is incredibly useful on mobile screens.
  2. Screen Reader Support (Accessibility): Visually impaired users rely on assistive technologies to navigate web forms. When a screen reader focuses on the checkbox, it looks for the linked <label> and reads aloud: "I agree to the terms and conditions, checkbox". Without the label, the screen reader might just say "checkbox, unselected," leaving the user with no idea what they are agreeing to.
  3. Focus: For text inputs (like an email or password field), clicking on the label text immediately snaps the typing cursor inside the text box so the user can start typing right away.
